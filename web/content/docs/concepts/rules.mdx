title: "Rules"

The rules, also called the mandate, are the owner's plain-English text saying who the agent may pay, in what, and how much. The model judges every payment against this text.

## Where they live

- **On-chain:** the wallet stores only the rules' sha256 hash, `mandate_hash`, readable with `get_config`.
- **The text:** stored by the Intented API, at most 2,000 characters, saved exactly as typed. Anyone can read it with `GET /api/mandates/{text_hash}` or on the web app's Rules page.

The wallet checks that the judge request carried the rules whose hash it holds. A verdict obtained with any other text is refused at `execute`.

<Warning>
  **The rules are public.** The chain holds only the hash, but the text is readable by anyone through the API and the web app. Don't put anything private in them.
</Warning>

## Writing them

The model applies what you write literally. Good rules name things exactly:

- **Who:** the exact NEAR account (`intented-vendor.testnet`) or, for Base, the exact address (`0x2ef2…49ef`). "The vendor" or "trusted sellers" leaves the model guessing.
- **In what:** the token, such as wNEAR or USDC.
- **How much:** a limit per call, in that token.

For example:

```text
Buy market data from intented-vendor.testnet, paid in wNEAR, at most 0.01 wNEAR per call,
or from 0x2ef2fed3f9b3d5385499008051031320b1fb49ef on Base, paid in USDC, at most 5 USDC per call.
```

The rules can only make the wallet stricter. A rule saying "up to 100 wNEAR" doesn't raise a 0.01 wNEAR per-payment cap: the contract refuses anything over its [hard limits](/docs/concepts/limits).

## Changing them

The owner changes the rules on the wallet's page: **Rules**, then **Edit**, then **Save and sign**. The page stores the new text with the API and asks the owner's NEAR wallet to sign `set_mandate` with its hash. The API judges each new proposal under the rules whose hash the wallet holds at that moment, so the change applies from the next payment. A verdict given under the old rules fails at `execute`, because its request carries the old hash.

<Note>
  If you run your own API, it must hold the text of the active rules, or it refuses to judge with `409`. Save the text once through that API's web app, or with `POST /api/mandate`.
</Note>
